Keep a tax payment moving across ten days
Jane owes a large tax bill. Raising the cash means analyzing her holdings, choosing a tax-efficient plan, obtaining approval, trading assets, waiting for settlement, and moving the money. Several people and systems participate. The task can span days even when no individual step requires days of computation.
Aman Singh calls this long-horizon work, distinguishing it from a model continuously generating tokens for hours. Financial services work is full of pauses, handoffs, and events outside the firm’s control. The challenge is remembering what should happen next and noticing when it becomes possible.
Arca carries out this work as a financial-services firm. Its agents take responsibility for keeping the process moving, bringing people in when the next step needs their judgment or action.

From an email to a shared plan
The example begins with an email: Jane needs $100,000 for taxes. Background agents read that message as one signal among many. Calls, documents, meetings, custodian events, and market changes can all provide information relevant to ongoing work.
The latest email leaves out information the agent needs:
The firm’s process:
how to handle this kind of request and who should participate.
Jane’s history:
including her earlier instruction never to sell her Apple position.
A place to do the analysis:
a sandbox for calculations, with tools and restrictions supplied by the agent’s harness.
Together, these let the agent prepare a plan that respects more than the dollar amount in the new message.
The result is a plan, but a plan cannot simply execute itself. Arca’s product gives the work a shared space where people can inspect it, discuss it with the agent, and perform the steps that require them. Multiple people can participate in the same work rather than holding separate private chats with incomplete context.
In Jane’s case, a person approves the plan and the outgoing email. Then the task waits for a response. Someone—or something—must keep track of whether Jane replied and what her answer changes.

Subscribe to what must happen next
Arca gives agents a way to register interest in future events. An agent can ask to be notified when Jane replies or when an ACH transfer lands. The signal system routes new events to the agents that care about them. Scheduled wakeups cover the case where the expected event never arrives.
At
2:00:01
, the agent declares which future events should wake it. Waiting does not require continuous model execution.
Real work rarely follows only the expected channel. Jane might never answer the email, but an in-person conversation could change the plan. When a colleague records that information in the product, the UI action becomes another signal. Rescheduling a calendar event or marking a task complete can do the same.
In the example, the updated information reduces the required cash from $100,000 to $80,000. A background agent passes the change to the agent responsible for the shared space, which updates the plan. Operational context then tells it when to involve the person who trades the assets.
The signal system therefore covers both the outside world and work inside the application. Persistent memory holds the state between events; the harness does the work when the agent wakes; collaboration brings in the appropriate person when a human action is due.
2:02:29
↗
Do not turn every future obligation into a task
With hundreds of signals arriving, coordination becomes a problem of its own. An agent can know that work remains and still pull in the wrong person, or pull in the right person too early. Arca has been improving the context agents receive, when they create tasks, and how they remember unfinished work.
Singh makes a useful distinction between work to remember and work a person can do now. Future obligations belong in the agent’s memory. A task should appear on a person’s plate when it is actionable. Otherwise the system merely replaces human bookkeeping with a noisy queue of premature requests.
Because Arca operates the service itself, it can also standardize how the firm works and incorporate that process into agent context. Colleagues’ explanations of why an action was right or wrong become evaluation material. That feedback helps refine both the agent and the workflow it follows.
The resulting interface changes who prompts whom. A person logs in to find tasks the agents have already prepared, after reading the relevant messages and documents. Agents bring people into the process at the point where their participation matters.
2:04:43
↗
Change the business goal, then the work
Singh ends by reconsidering the initial email. Why did Jane have to ask about the tax bill at all? A proactive firm would prepare for it before the client raised the issue.
He proposes a new business goal: every January, analyze each client’s tax situation and prepare the outreach. With the same signal, memory, analysis, and collaboration primitives, agents can drive that goal and bring prepared plans and approval-ready emails to the team.
Singh’s ambition is to steer the firm by changing its agents’ goals. For Jane, the difference would be concrete: a prepared tax plan arrives before she has to ask for one. A person still approves the outreach, but no person has to remember to start the process.
