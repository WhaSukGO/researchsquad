# Receptive fields, binocular interaction and functional architecture in the cat's visual cortex

Date: 1962
Kind: paraphrase
Source: D. H. Hubel and T. N. Wiesel, Journal of Physiology 160:106-154, 1962

[Paraphrase, not verbatim.] Recording single cells in the primary visual cortex of the cat, Hubel and Wiesel found
that cortical cells respond to stimuli in a limited region of the visual field (the receptive field), and that most
cells are selective for the orientation of a line or edge. They distinguished two classes. "Simple" cells have
receptive fields with separate excitatory and inhibitory subregions, so that a correctly oriented bar at a precise
position excites the cell; their behaviour can be explained by summing the inputs of a few lateral geniculate
cells with aligned receptive-field centres. "Complex" cells respond to a correctly oriented bar anywhere within a
larger region: their response is largely independent of the exact position of the stimulus. The authors proposed
a hierarchical scheme in which complex cells are built by pooling the outputs of several simple cells that share
the same orientation preference but have receptive fields at neighbouring positions. Cells with similar
preferences are grouped in columns across the cortical surface.
