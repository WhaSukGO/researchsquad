# Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities

Date: 1982
Kind: paraphrase
Source: J. J. Hopfield, Proc. National Academy of Sciences 79:2554-2558, 1982

[Paraphrase, not verbatim.] Hopfield described a fully connected network of binary threshold units with symmetric
weights and asynchronous updates, and showed that it has an energy function that never increases, so the
dynamics converge to stable states. Choosing the weights as a sum of outer products of the patterns to be stored
(a Hebbian rule) makes those patterns local minima of the energy: the network acts as a content-addressable
memory that recovers a stored pattern from a partial or corrupted cue. He reported numerical experiments on the
storage capacity (about 0.15 N patterns for N units) and connected the model to the statistical physics of spin
glasses, which drew many physicists into neural network research in the following years.
